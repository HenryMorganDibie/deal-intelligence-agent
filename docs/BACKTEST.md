# Historical backtest

Walk-forward replay over **2024-01-15 to 2025-06-15**: 46 companies (US, JSE, NGX, NSE Kenya) x 18 monthly as-of dates. On each date the engine sees only sources published in the previous 30 days, and each prediction is judged on the 90 days that followed. Generated by `python main.py backtest --historical`; raw predictions in `data/historical_backtest.json`.

**Reading the table.** *Precision*: share of labelled predictions followed by a confirming SEC filing, exchange disclosure or credible report (strictly after the as-of date). *Official*: the same, counting only SEC filings and exchange disclosures, which rules out follow-up news about the same story. *Base rate*: share of all company-months followed by that event anyway. *Lift*: precision / base rate, how much better a flag is than picking a company-month at random. *Recall*: share of confirmed events a prediction preceded. *Hit rate*: price moved in the predicted direction net of the market (10 days). Earnings surprises and insider trades are judged on price only.

## Method and caveats

- Detection in the replay uses SEC filings, NGX disclosures and date-restricted Google News. The live product also reads ~50 African RSS feeds and JSE SENS, which have no searchable history, so live coverage is broader than the replay's.
- Ground truth is limited to what the sources record. An event reported only by outlets below the credibility bar counts as not confirmed, which biases precision down, not up.
- NGX and NSE Kenya have no free per-stock price history, so African hit rates cover JSE names only; NGX/NSE price outcomes accumulate from the daily snapshot going forward.
- M&A is confirmed by completion or by a live deal's regulated milestones (merger proxy, Form 425 business-combination communications, tender offer filings, shareholder or regulatory approval, definitive agreement), since large deals rarely close within the 90-day horizon. Base rates use the same events.
- Static calibration only: adaptive calibration, which learns from outcomes, is off so that later results cannot leak into earlier predictions.
